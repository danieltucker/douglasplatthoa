These funds are budgeted as a ceiling — money is only spent if a situation actually requires it. Whatever isn't spent by year-end rolls into the association's reserves rather than being refunded or carried as a loss. However, if usage is high in 2026, these allocations may need to increase in future years to adequately cover the association's needs.

Legal / Accounting covers attorney fees, CPA costs, or other professional services if needed — for example, reviewing governing documents, handling a collections dispute, or filing tax returns. If the year is uneventful, this line may go largely unspent.

Reserve for Future Repairs is seed money for the HOA's long-term repair fund. The stormwater pond and any shared infrastructure will eventually need maintenance beyond routine upkeep — this begins building that cushion so the cost doesn't fall entirely on a future special assessment.

2027 Budget Outlook

Our best hope for 2027 is that the base budget drops to approximately $21,320 with quarterly dues around $56.11/owner — but this is dependent on how contingency funds are used in 2026 and what the overall financial outlook looks like heading into next year. It's an optimistic target, not a guarantee. Dues could remain closer to the 2026 level or potentially higher depending on circumstances.

What's expected to be removed: The $1,235 Management Company Onboarding & Setup Fee is a one-time cost Windermere charges to establish the association in their system — setting up bank accounts, onboarding owners into the portal, and configuring all records from scratch. This only applies in the first year. The contingency lines — Legal/Accounting ($6,000) and Reserve for Future Repairs ($1,445) — may also be reduced if 2026 ends with those funds largely unspent, since the board will have real spending data to base 2027 allocations on rather than first-year estimates.

Why items might not be removed: If the HOA board votes to change management companies at renewal (January 2027), a new company would likely charge a similar or higher setup fee. If legal issues arise in 2026 — such as collections disputes or document revisions — the board may keep the legal budget at or above $6,000. Similarly, if the reserve fund is drawn on for a repair, the board may choose to increase reserve contributions in 2027 to rebuild that cushion faster.

The 2027 budget will be drafted by Windermere and submitted for board review and ratification before the year begins.
Note: Per Washington HOA law, this budget is considered ratified unless 50%+1 of voting members vote to reject it, regardless of whether a quorum is present.

Your HOA Board — Roles & Responsibilities

While Windermere handles day-to-day operations, the HOA Board of Directors retains ultimate authority — setting direction, approving spending, and ensuring the association operates in the best interests of all 95 homeowners.

⚖️ Governance & Decision-Making

  • Adopt and amend CC&Rs, bylaws, and community rules
  • Set policies for conduct, common areas, and homeowner expectations
  • Ratify all vendor and service contracts
  • Ratify or reject the annual operating budget
  • Hold at least 4 board meetings + 1 general membership meeting per year

💰 Financial Oversight

  • Review and approve the annual budget; set quarterly assessments
  • Authorize withdrawals from the reserve fund for major repairs
  • Approve any special assessments if the budget is insufficient
  • Ensure adequate General Liability and D&O insurance is maintained

🚀 First-Year Priorities

  • Establish association bank accounts
  • Distribute CC&Rs, bylaws, and rules to all 95 homeowners
  • Seat a full board with elected officers
  • Notify all homeowners of the 2026 dues schedule and payment options
  • Set up email list, owner portal access, and mailing procedures
  • Confirm all required insurance policies are active
Windermere handles: Day-to-day operations, collections, record-keeping, and reporting.
The HOA Board decides: Policies, budget approval, major spending, and community direction.
